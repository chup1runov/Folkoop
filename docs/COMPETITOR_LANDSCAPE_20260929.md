# FOLKOOP — competitor landscape

Date: 2026-09-29
Status: public research note. External products change over time; re-check current features before using this document for formal claims.

## Why these projects matter

FOLKOOP sits at the intersection of several existing categories rather than one conventional market:
- community coordination;
- mutual aid / need-offer exchange;
- project collaboration;
- civic participation and city navigation;
- local social networks and offline events;
- cooperative governance;
- collective finance;
- marketplaces / shared resources.

No single item below should be described as a complete equivalent of FOLKOOP. The useful comparison is which part of the FOLKOOP system each one already solves well.

## Priority competitors / benchmarks

### 1. Hylo
Official: https://www.hylo.com/

Closest overall product benchmark found so far.

Relevant areas:
- people and communities;
- needs and offers;
- skills;
- projects and working groups;
- events;
- place-based organizing;
- collective decisions and participatory funding;
- coordination over engagement.

Why study it:
Hylo is the strongest benchmark for the core FOLKOOP idea that communities need tools to coordinate real action rather than only consume a feed.

### 2. Karrot
Official: https://karrot.world/
Docs/community: https://docs.karrot.world/

Relevant areas:
- grassroots groups;
- local self-organization;
- sharing/resources;
- activities/tasks;
- real-world participation;
- democratic community operation.

Why study it:
Karrot is a strong benchmark for the transition from digital coordination to face-to-face local action.

### 3. Decidim
Official: https://decidim.org/
Docs: https://docs.decidim.org/

Relevant areas:
- participatory democracy;
- proposals;
- meetings;
- deliberation;
- initiatives;
- participatory budgeting;
- accountability;
- civic processes.

Why study it:
Decidim is a mature benchmark for the FOLKOOP City/governance direction.

### 4. Nextdoor
Official: https://nextdoor.com/

Relevant areas:
- neighbourhood social graph;
- hyperlocal communities;
- recommendations;
- local groups;
- local businesses;
- place-based discovery.

Why study it:
Nextdoor demonstrates how strong local-density effects can emerge when a network is tied to real neighbourhoods.

### 5. Geneva
Official: https://www.geneva.com/

Relevant areas:
- group chat;
- local group discovery;
- events and meetups;
- audio/video community interaction;
- real-world social connection.

Why study it:
Geneva is a useful benchmark for People + Communities + offline events, especially for onboarding people into local activity.

### 6. TimeRepublik
Official: https://timerepublik.com/

Relevant areas:
- need/help exchange;
- skills;
- time-banking;
- communities;
- peer-to-peer contribution.

Why study it:
It is close to the simple FOLKOOP "I need / I can" exchange pattern and offers a different incentive mechanism based on time.

### 7. Open Collective
Official: https://opencollective.com/
Fiscal hosting: https://opencollective.com/fiscal-hosting
Docs: https://documentation.opencollective.com/

Relevant areas:
- collective/project finance;
- donations and grants;
- transparent budgets;
- expenses;
- fiscal hosting;
- collective financial administration.

Why study it:
Open Collective is a strong benchmark for what could eventually happen after a FOLKOOP Project becomes a real group that needs money, reporting and financial infrastructure.

### 8. Loomio
Official: https://www.loomio.com/
Decision-making: https://www.loomio.com/collaborative-decision-making/
Docs: https://www.loomio.com/docs/

Relevant areas:
- discussion;
- proposals;
- polls;
- consent/decision processes;
- persistent decision records;
- governance.

Why study it:
Loomio is a useful benchmark for future cooperative/member governance inside FOLKOOP.

### 9. Sharetribe
Official: https://www.sharetribe.com/

Relevant areas:
- marketplaces;
- profiles/listings;
- product/service/resource exchange;
- rentals;
- transactions;
- payments;
- messaging;
- marketplace infrastructure.

Why study it:
Sharetribe is a benchmark for the economic/resource-exchange side of FOLKOOP, especially if shared resources or supplier/marketplace functions deepen.

## Working proximity view

### Closest to FOLKOOP as a whole
1. Hylo
2. Karrot
3. Nextdoor / Geneva, depending on use case

### Closest to specific layers
- People / community: Hylo, Nextdoor, Geneva
- Need / Offer: Hylo, TimeRepublik
- Projects / collective work: Hylo, Karrot
- City / civic participation: Decidim, Nextdoor
- Offline/community action: Karrot, Geneva, Hylo
- Governance: Loomio, Decidim, Hylo
- Collective finance: Open Collective
- Marketplace/resources: Sharetribe

## FOLKOOP differentiation hypothesis

The current differentiation hypothesis is not that any individual feature is new.

The hypothesis is the connected architecture:

**People -> Cooperate -> City -> Center -> Action -> Outcome**

with:
- a cooperation graph rather than a follower graph as the main organizing idea;
- projects as structured cooperation objects;
- City as the civic/opportunity layer;
- Center as the physical/community layer;
- Mura as a human-facing guide/interface layer;
- SDCF as a non-runtime semantic/provenance/outcome-integrity bridge.

This remains a hypothesis to validate, not a claim that no other product in the world overlaps with the same combination.

## Research order

If time is limited, study in this order:
1. Hylo
2. Karrot
3. Decidim
4. Open Collective
5. Loomio
6. Nextdoor
7. Geneva
8. TimeRepublik
9. Sharetribe

For each competitor, record:
- core user;
- primary object (post, group, project, request, proposal, transaction, event);
- onboarding;
- discovery/matching;
- coordination;
- governance;
- offline link;
- business/funding model;
- open-source/licensing status;
- strongest feature FOLKOOP should learn from;
- feature/strategy FOLKOOP should avoid copying blindly.
