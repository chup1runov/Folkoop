## What the earlier feature inventory means

| Source-review terminology | Scope recorded in the earlier review | FOLKOOP design implication, not a quotation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One digital information field | News, documents, meeting results, personal account, feedback and service information | Home should connect information to a next action rather than merely maximize scrolling. |
| Joint economic activity | Joint buying AND selling, producers, suppliers, demand, distribution and logistics | A purchase coordinator alone does not cover collaborative production or collective sales. |
| Local self-organization | Residents' problems, votes, opinions, shared improvements and local-budget participation | Discussion, decision and implementation need distinct records. A poll is not automatically a legally valid assembly. |
| Community operations | Dues, reports, meters, applications and accounting integrations | Durable community work needs documents and clear responsibilities, not just ephemeral chat. |
| Land and resource discovery | Maps, plots, neighbors, economic activity and settlement communication | Geography can organize cooperation; do not expose exact residential location by default. |
| Education and coordinators | Courses, pilots, academy and coordinator plan | Human facilitation is part of the model, not something software alone proves it can replace. |

## Design conclusions for FOLKOOP

These are our implementation choices, not promises made by Tokarenko:

A useful Home begins with actions requiring a response, then active cooperation and bounded recent publications. Guest Home must explain where to start without pretending that personal data has loaded. The application must keep cooperation broader than shopping: people, mutual help, projects, city opportunities and the offline Center all remain first-class.

The next conceptual gap is collective decision-making and continuity: an advisory proposal/poll, a recorded decision, assigned follow-up and a visible outcome. This review does NOT add voting, legal membership, municipal-budget administration, pooled money, a payment service or collective ownership of the platform. Those are separate design and authorization decisions.

Current code implements only part of the wider cooperation idea. Existing RLS and tests are not proof of adoption or a successful cooperative economy. A real pilot should measure completed useful actions and return participation rather than present planned capabilities as social impact.
