# FOLKOOP — cooperative and organizational strategy note

29 September 2026.

Status: public planning note. This document records product/organization decisions that are safe to keep in the public repository. It does **not** define a final legal structure and is not legal, tax or funding advice.

## Why this note exists

FOLKOOP has converged from several earlier directions into one product:

- a social/cooperation network;
- the former Sverinav civic-navigation work, now represented by **City**;
- the FOLKUNO physical/community-space direction, now represented by **Center**.

The product thesis is already defined in `docs/PRODUCT_CONCEPT.md`. This note adds the organizational consequence: the product layers should not be confused with legal entities.

## Public product structure

A useful shorthand is:

**People -> Cooperate -> City -> Center -> Action -> Outcome**

### People

Profiles, skills, communities, communication and opt-in discovery.

### Cooperate

Needs, offers, shared resources, shared purchases, projects, teams, tasks and other coordination objects.

### City

The civic/opportunity layer inherited from Sverinav. Its role is discovery, navigation and routing toward relevant public, civic and local resources without pretending to be the authority itself.

### Center

The physical/community layer inherited from FOLKUNO: real-world meeting places, Hosts, events, local onboarding and partner connections. A permanent operating venue is not assumed by the current software pilot.

### Action and outcome

The intended endpoint is useful activity outside the interface. The product should distinguish coordination records from independently verified real-world outcomes.

## One product, specialized layers

The current direction is **not** to maintain three competing products, identities or user bases.

FOLKOOP should instead reuse common identity, cooperation and data infrastructure where that is justified while keeping clear functional boundaries:

- People supplies participants;
- Cooperate turns intent into commitments and coordination;
- City connects people to existing city and civic resources;
- Center can create local density and turn digital relationships into real-world activity.

This is consistent with the current FOLKOOP product concept and migration history.

## Göteborg-first organizational principle

Göteborg remains the first environment in which to validate the model.

The organization should be designed around evidence from a narrow pilot rather than around an assumed future international structure.

Relevant validation questions include:

- Can real needs/offers produce useful matches?
- Do commitments lead to real-world action?
- Does City routing help users reach useful external resources?
- Does a physical/community layer materially improve local density and repeat cooperation?
- Which activities actually require a formal organization, staff, premises or external funding?

## Legal structure is intentionally unresolved

No final public claim is made yet about whether FOLKOOP should ultimately use:

- one legal entity;
- a cooperative/member-owned entity;
- a nonprofit/association structure;
- a company;
- multiple cooperating entities.

The legal form should follow the actual functions, funding routes, liability, governance and ownership requirements demonstrated by the pilot.

A key design rule is therefore:

**Do not map each product module mechanically to a separate legal entity.**

The boundaries of People, Cooperate, City and Center are product boundaries first.

## Cooperative governance questions to validate

The following questions are appropriate for external cooperative/business advisers and can be discussed publicly:

1. At what point does a member-owned or cooperative structure create real user value rather than administrative overhead?
2. Which decisions genuinely benefit from member democracy, and which require delegated product/operational responsibility?
3. How should ordinary users, active contributors and formal members differ?
4. How can local community participation coexist with a coherent cross-city product standard?
5. Which legal structure best supports a Göteborg pilot before the model is proven?
6. How should public-benefit activity be separated from commercial activity if both emerge?
7. Which funding sources fit each type of activity without distorting the product mission?
8. What governance structure prevents both founder over-control and decision paralysis?
9. How can the model expand to another city without rebuilding the organization from zero?
10. Which organizational decisions can safely be deferred until after pilot evidence exists?

## Adviser route

Two relevant Göteborg-area adviser tracks identified during planning are:

- **Coompanion Göteborgsregionen** for cooperative/shared-enterprise organization and governance;
- **Business Region Göteborg** for broader business-model, market, financing and ecosystem questions.

Exact programmes, eligibility, pricing and availability are time-dependent and must be rechecked before relying on them.

## Public/private boundary

Public GitHub should contain:

- product purpose and architecture;
- public pilot assumptions;
- open governance questions;
- public technical and product decisions;
- evidence that is safe and useful for contributors.

Private project records should contain detailed working material whose publication could create unnecessary legal, commercial, privacy or negotiation risk, including:

- detailed ownership scenarios;
- founder/IP allocation hypotheses;
- compensation and revenue-allocation options;
- non-public consultant preparation;
- negotiation positions;
- private correspondence;
- credentials, invite codes and personal identifiers.

This repository remains the public source of truth for product/code state. Private records must not silently override public implementation facts.

## Current decision

For now:

1. continue one FOLKOOP product and repository;
2. preserve City as the Sverinav-derived civic layer;
3. preserve Center as the FOLKUNO-derived physical/community layer;
4. validate the Göteborg core loop before adding organizational complexity;
5. use external cooperative/business advice to test legal and governance options;
6. keep sensitive ownership/IP/compensation working hypotheses outside the public repository;
7. document a final legal structure only after it is actually chosen.
